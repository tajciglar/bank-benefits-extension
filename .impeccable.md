# Design Context — Bank Card Benefits Notifier

This file captures the design context for all future UI/UX work on this project.
Update it when the audience, brand, or aesthetic direction evolves.

## Design Context

### Users

**Tech-savvy Slovenian adults, ages 20–35.** Product Hunt / Hacker News-adjacent.
They already install browser extensions for a living, have strong opinions about
design, and instantly recognize AI-template aesthetics. They use this extension
while shopping online — the popup is a quick check-in ("which cards should I
use here?"), and the in-page notification is a low-key heads-up that fires on a
merchant page they're already browsing.

The job they're trying to get done: **stop missing discounts they're entitled
to without having to remember 16 different bank benefit programs.** They didn't
choose this extension for its UI — they chose it because it solves a real
problem in Slovenia, where benefit info is fragmented across many bank PDFs.
But they will *judge* it for its UI, and if it looks generic they'll question
whether the data behind it is curated thoughtfully too.

### Brand Personality

**Three words: warm, considered, quietly clever.**

The tone is *"a friend texting you a deal"* — knowledgeable, low-key, never
salesy or anxious. The friend in question knows banking inside out, doesn't
oversell, and trusts you to act on the info.

Emotional goals:
- **Trust** (this is about money — must feel reliable, not like a coupon scam)
- **Mild delight** (when a notification fires, the user should feel "huh, nice")
- **Confidence** (the design itself signals that the data is curated, not scraped sloppily)

What the brand is NOT:
- Not corporate-banking serious. Not Honey/Rakuten salesy.
- Not gamified or playful in a juvenile way (no confetti, no streaks).
- Not aggressively branded — the merchants and discounts are the stars; the extension is the host who quietly walked over with a tray.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Editorial warmth with restrained personality.** Think a well-designed Slovenian
publication or a thoughtful indie product more than a SaaS app.

- **Theme:** Both light and dark, switched via `prefers-color-scheme`.
  Dark mode must be a *real* design with its own palette decisions, not an
  inverted light mode.
- **Typography:** Distinctive serif or transitional display face for headings
  paired with a refined sans for UI. Avoid Inter, Roboto, Segoe UI defaults.
  Consider: Söhne, GT America, Inter Tight; or paired with a serif like
  Tiempos, Söhne Mono, or a Slovenian-friendly face that handles š/č/ž
  glyphs well.
- **Color:** Warm neutrals with one confident accent. Move *away* from the
  current cream-and-forest-green palette — it's pleasant but reads as a
  template. Tint neutrals toward a single brand hue for subconscious cohesion.
- **Layout:** Asymmetric, breathing space, intentional use of grid breaks.
  Don't wrap everything in cards. Don't use eyebrow tags. Don't stack
  identical stat tiles.
- **Motion:** Sparing and exponential. One well-orchestrated load reveal beats
  many micro-interactions. No bounce, no elastic.

**Visual references to consider** (not to copy):
- Linear's restraint, but warmer
- Are.na / The Browser's editorial typography
- A well-designed Slovenian poster or magazine spread (Studio Marsi,
  Mladinska Knjiga book covers)
- The Browser Company's Arc — playful but mature

**Explicit anti-references:**
- **No SaaS dashboards.** No Vercel/Stripe/Linear template look. No gradient
  CTA buttons. No 4-up metric tiles with big numbers.
- **No bank websites.** No corporate blues, no stock photography, no glossy
  gradients. Don't look like nlb.si or otpbanka.si.
- **No coupon sites.** No red SAVE badges, no percentage-off stickers, no
  Retailmenot/Honey/Rakuten cluttered card grids.
- **No "AI slop" fingerprints**: pill eyebrow tags, hero stat cards, lift-on-hover
  transforms applied to every interactive element, glassmorphism, drop-shadow rounded
  rectangles, purple-to-blue gradients, decorative monospace fonts, generic icons-over-headings.

### Design Principles

1. **Earn personality through typography and spacing, not chrome.**
   No badges, eyebrows, or decorative containers exist purely to add visual
   interest. If something is on the screen, it carries information.

2. **Warmth without cuteness.**
   Friendly tone, but the visuals must hold up to a designer's eye. No emoji
   spam, no cartoon rounded shapes, no overly soft pastels.

3. **Reward the discerning eye.**
   Micro-details matter: a custom checkmark animation, asymmetric grid moments,
   thoughtful Slovenian quotation marks („…"), correct š/č/ž rendering, a
   notification that feels handmade. These cost little but signal care.

4. **Be specifically Slovenian without being touristy.**
   Don't put Triglav silhouettes or alpine landscapes anywhere. The Slovenian
   character comes through in: language nuance, currency formatting (€ before
   number, comma as decimal), respect for proper diacritics, and a restraint
   that feels more Central European than American startup.

5. **Notifications are gifts, not banners.**
   The in-page notification should feel like a tasteful nudge from a friend,
   not a marketing toast. Quiet entrance, easy dismiss, never auto-disappear
   in a way that makes the user feel ambushed.

6. **Two real themes, not one and a half.**
   Dark mode is not "the same design with inverted colors." It's its own
   palette, its own contrast decisions, its own personality within the same
   brand.
